## Hardware: mixer latency on the Mac mini (scripts/bench/mixer_bench.c, raw: mixer-bench-m4.txt)

Batch 1, one thread, fp32, only the parts that differ between layouts (address projections, static read, fast
state update and read). Relative to S-merged (run.c's single U):

- Merging static and fast into one U buys no consistent speed over a split layout fused into one pass: fused
  split runs at 0.96x to 1.16x of merged. The 1.16x at 42M repeated in two runs, so merged is somewhat faster in
  that shape, and fused split is faster at 110M. Both move the same bytes: run.c reads mu every step to
  restore the prior.
- The lazy scalar decay saves nothing measurable either; the loop is bound by memory, not the multiply.
- Separate addresses with the same slots (P-free) cost 1.26x at 260K and 1.35x to 1.41x at 15M to 110M: a second
  key projection and a second read. That is the only speed advantage of the unified block in this test.
- Equal-parameter separated layouts are as fast or faster: P(105,70) 0.82x, P(534,356) 0.80x, A 1.00x.
- At 110M the merged layout runs at about 67 GB/s, near a single M4 core's bandwidth, so this is the
  memory-bound regime that decoding on small hardware sees.

The first, unvectorized build showed merged 1.5x faster than split; that came from scalar float reductions
and from a split loop making two passes, and is not a property of the layouts.
