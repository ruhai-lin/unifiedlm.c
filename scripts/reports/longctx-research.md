# Long-context and mid-range (64–1K tokens) techniques: survey for the SG/SD fixed-state model

Date: 2026-11-10. arXiv ids marked (v) I checked by web search during this task. Ids without (v) come from my own memory; I am confident in them unless they say "unsure". "Applies?" is my judgement for a 15M/42M model trained at ctx 1024 with an m×d slot memory, a per-slot decay λ_t = exp(-rate·g(x_t)), and g = softplus(rank-16 low-rank(address)).

Repo constraint (AGENTS.md): any change has to be mirrored in kernel.py, step/forward_recurrent, run.c and export.py, so "cost" below counts all four. A change confined to the gate function is small. A new operator (attention, conv state) is large.

---

## Diagnosis first (what the literature says your symptoms mean)

- **Fixed-state models losing to attention at mid-range is the expected recall gap.** It is not a bug. Zoology (Arora et al. 2023, arXiv 2312.04927) and Based (2402.18668) show that associative recall in gated recurrences scales with state size, and that a Transformer's KV cache wins once the number of things to recall grows. "Repeat After Me" (Jelassi et al. 2024, 2402.01032) proves copying needs state that grows with length. Expect to narrow the 64–1K gap, not close it, without some attention.
- **Learned horizons of 10–60 tokens are probably partly an init/parameterization artifact.** Mainstream linear RNNs do not leave horizons to a plain gate. They hard-code a spread of timescales at init (Mamba/Mamba2 dt log-uniform, Griffin a∈[0.9,0.999], RetNet fixed multi-scale γ, GLA temperature 1/16, HGRN lower bounds). A gate made of softplus(low-rank(·)) with no per-slot bias and no temperature starts at an O(1) decay rate. That is a horizon of a few tokens, and gradients for longer horizons are weak, especially under a 1024-token training window. **Unsure:** I don't know your current rate init. Check it first.
- **No collapse at 16K is consistent with short horizons.** Stuffed Mamba (2410.07145 (v)) finds that collapse comes from models that never learned to forget. Your model forgets aggressively, so it extrapolates flat but under-uses the mid-range. That tradeoff is exactly what the gate design controls.

## A) Transformer techniques

Most of these fix the *Transformer's* length extrapolation. They matter to you mainly (i) for a fair baseline, and (ii) as ideas you can borrow (forget gates, sinks, document masking, length curricula).

| Technique | What / fixes | Key result | Applies to SG/SD? | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Position Interpolation / NTK-aware / YaRN** | Rescale RoPE frequencies so a model trained at L runs at s·L. YaRN also scales attention temperature by 0.1·ln s+1. | PI: Chen et al. 2023, arXiv 2306.15595. NTK-aware: reddit/bloc97 2023, no paper. YaRN: Peng et al. 2023, 2309.00071. Extends Llama 2 to 64–128K with about 0.1% of the pretraining tokens. | No. You have no RoPE. Use only if the Transformer baseline is compared beyond 1024. | n/a |
| **LongRoPE** | Searches non-uniform per-dimension RoPE rescaling and does progressive extension. | Ding et al. 2024, 2402.13753. 2M context. | No (baseline only). | n/a |
| **ALiBi** | Linear distance penalty in the logits, with fixed geometric slopes per head. | Press et al. 2021/ICLR'22, 2108.12409. Train short, test long. | Conceptually yes. ALiBi is a *fixed, data-independent* decay with **geometric slopes across heads**, which is a multi-timescale prior. Its lesson: hard-code a spread of horizons. | n/a directly. The idea goes into the gate init (B1). |
| **Attention sinks / StreamingLLM** | Keep the first few tokens in the KV cache, because softmax dumps mass there. | Xiao et al. 2023, 2309.17453. Stable streaming to 4M tokens. | Weak. RNNs have no softmax-sink pathology. The analog is a **learned initial state** (Hymba "meta tokens", 2411.13676 (v)), which gives the model a "prior" to read when nothing relevant is in memory. Your SG/SD already anchor at an FFN prior, which plays this role. | n/a |
| **Gated attention (output gate)** | Head-wise sigmoid gate on the attention output. Removes sinks, adds non-linearity, stabilizes training. | Qiu et al. 2025, 2505.06708 (v). NeurIPS'25 oral. Used in Qwen3-Next. | **Yes.** Gate the memory read: y = read ⊙ σ(W x). GLA/GDN/Mamba2 all do this. | Small (one projection, elementwise). Must be mirrored in run.c. |
| **Forgetting Transformer (FoX)** | Adds a data-dependent scalar forget gate to softmax attention: logits += Σ log f. No RoPE needed. | Lin et al. ICLR'25, 2503.02130 (v). Beats Transformer on long-ctx LM and extrapolation, and keeps the Transformer's long-ctx retrieval over Mamba2/HGRN2/DeltaNet. The "Pro" block adds an output gate, output norm, QK-norm and a KV-shift (a short conv). | It's the attention-side twin of your λ. Two things transfer: (1) the Pro-block extras (output gate/norm, KV-shift) help *both* attention and recurrent models in their ablations; (2) FoX is the natural "matched Transformer + forget gate" baseline. | n/a as a model change. The Pro-block extras are small to medium. |
| **Stick-breaking attention** | Each token takes a fraction β of the remaining mass, nearest first. That gives an implicit recency bias with no positional encoding. | Tan et al. ICLR'25, 2410.17980 (v). Matches or beats RoPE at 1B/3B, and better on RULER and length generalization. | No (it's an attention variant). | n/a |
| **Log-length scaling (logN / SSMax)** | Multiply logits by s·log n so attention doesn't flatten as n grows. | Nakanishi 2025, 2501.19399 (v). The same trick appears in Qwen "logn attn" and in Chiang & Cholak 2022 (2202.12172). | No softmax. **Loose analog:** SG's running mean is a normalized average whose effective number of items grows with horizon. An analogous "sharpening" would be a temperature on the address. Speculative. | n/a |
| **QK-norm** | RMSNorm on q, k. Stabilizes logits and allows a higher LR. | Henry et al. 2020 (2010.04245). Dehghani et al. ViT-22B 2023 (2302.05442). OLMo 2, Gemma 3. | Yes, partly. GDN/DeltaNet L2-normalize q and k. This is **required for the delta rule to be stable** (β‖k‖²≤2). If SD doesn't normalize the address/key, do so. | Tiny. |
| **Document masking / intra-doc attention** | Don't attend across packed-document boundaries. | Zhao et al. 2024, "Analysing the impact of sequence composition", 2402.13991. Llama 3 used it. It improves LM and long-ctx quality. | **Yes, important.** The RNN version is a **state reset at EOS** (FLA's cu_seqlens / varlen). If FineWeb-Edu docs are packed into 1024 windows without a reset, then whenever the doc changes, long memory is *noise*. That pushes the gate toward short horizons. | Small. Reset λ→0 / m→prior at the boundary token. It touches the kernel (varlen or masking λ). |
| **Progressive length training** | Train short, then extend to longer sequences (with RoPE base change). | Xiong et al. "Effective Long-Context Scaling" 2023, 2309.16039. Gao et al. ProLong 2024, 2410.02660. GrowLength 2310.00576. | **Yes, via the RNN analog (B: state passing/TBTT).** Gradients never see dependencies longer than 1024, and few near 1024. | Compute grows with length (linear for the RNN). |
| **Sliding window + global layers** | Interleave local SWA and full attention layers. | Mistral 7B 2310.06825 (SWA). Gemma 2 2408.00118 (1:1, 4K window). Gemma 3 2503.19786 (5:1, 1K window). | Only as a hybrid (see B, hybrids). | Large. |

## B) Linear-RNN / SSM techniques

### B1. Decay/gate parameterization and init (directly about λ)

| Technique | What / fixes | Key result | Applies? | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mamba/Mamba2 Δ and A init** | Δ = softplus(W x + dt_bias), with dt_bias = softplus⁻¹(dt) and dt ~ log-uniform[1e-3, 1e-1]. A ~ U[1,16] per head (stored as A_log). Decay = exp(-Δ·A). A **per-channel/head bias plus log-uniform init** spreads horizons over about 1–1000 tokens. | Gu & Dao 2023 (2312.00752). Dao & Gu 2024 (2405.21060). Gated DeltaNet uses the identical gate. | **Highly applicable.** Your g = softplus(lowrank(x)) lacks a per-slot additive bias, so every slot starts at about the same rate. Add `b_slot` (init = softplus⁻¹ of a log-uniform rate) inside the softplus, and/or make `rate` per-slot log-uniform. | **Tiny.** A parameter and an init. Mirror in kernel/step/run.c/export. Zero compute. |
| **Griffin RG-LRU init** | a = σ(Λ)^{c·r_t}, with c=8. Λ is initialized so that a^c ~ U[0.9, 0.999], i.e. horizons of 10–1000. | De et al. 2024, 2402.19427. Hawk/Griffin match Llama-2 with fewer tokens. | Same lesson as above. It also shows a **fixed exponent c=8** that sharpens the data-dependent part. | Tiny. |
| **GLA gate temperature** | α_t = σ(x W₁W₂ + b)^{1/τ} with **rank-16 low-rank W₁W₂ and τ=16**. That is log α = logsigmoid(·)/16, which pushes decays toward 1 (slow forgetting). | Yang et al. 2023/ICML'24, 2312.06635. FLA's `gate_logit_normalizer=16`. | **Your gate is basically GLA's (rank-16 low-rank), but without the 1/16 temperature or a bias.** GLA's authors added τ specifically to encourage slow forgetting. Try g = softplus(lowrank)/τ, or equivalently init `rate` 16× smaller. | **Tiny.** |
| **HGRN lower-bounded forget gate, increasing with depth** | f = γ_l + (1-γ_l)·σ(·), where γ_l is learnable and monotonically increasing across layers (cumsum of softmax). Lower layers stay local, upper layers go long. | Qin, Yang, Zhong NeurIPS'23, 2311.04823 (v). The ablation's biggest gain is the monotone lower bound. HGRN2 2404.07904 adds state expansion via an outer product. | **Applicable, and it matches your finding** that a few layers already reach 150–350. Force it: λ ≥ λ_min(l). In exp-form: rate_eff = rate·g·(1-…) or clamp horizon ≥ H_l with H_l growing with depth. | Small. Per-layer parameters plus a formula change. |
| **RetNet multi-scale fixed decay** | Per-head fixed γ_h = 1 - 2^{-5-h}. | Sun et al. 2023, 2307.08621. | Use as a **floor** or init grid for slots, rather than as fixed values. | Tiny. |
| **Channel-wise (fine-grained) decay** | KDA (Kimi Delta Attention) gives GDN a per-channel decay instead of a per-head scalar. | Kimi Linear 2025, 2510.26692 (v). 3:1 KDA:MLA hybrid that beats full attention in their comparisons. | You already have per-slot decay, which is the fine-grained version. KDA validates keeping it. KDA computes its gate from x with a low-rank projection like GLA (I believe; **mark unsure**). | n/a |
| **Gate from hidden x vs from key/address** | GLA, Mamba2, GDN, KDA, FoX and mLSTM compute the decay from the **layer input x** via a separate projection. They don't compute it from the normalized key. | Convention across all of the above. **I know of no clean ablation** isolating it. | Plausible small win. The address may be normalized or sparse in ways that make it a poor carrier of "how long to remember". The cost is the same rank-16 parameters. **Unsure of the effect size.** | Tiny. |
| **Mamba-3** | Exponential-trapezoidal discretization, complex (rotary) state, MIMO. With B/C biases the short conv becomes unnecessary. | Lahoti et al. ICLR'26, 2603.15569 (v). Matches Mamba-2 perplexity at half the state. | The trapezoidal update (mixing x_t and x_{t-1} into the write) is a cheap "built-in short conv". The complex/rotary state is a PaTH-like position signal. Medium to large to port. | Medium. |

### B2. Write rule, state capacity, read normalization

| Technique | What / fixes | Key result | Applies? | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **DeltaNet / Gated DeltaNet** | Delta rule (erase-then-write by key) plus a Mamba2-style gate. Fixes interference in the fixed state, so the model can keep a longer horizon without the state filling with junk. | Yang et al. 2024 (2406.06484). Gated DeltaNet ICLR'25 (2412.06464). Beats Mamba2/DeltaNet on recall and long-ctx. Used in Qwen3-Next (3:1 with attention). | This is your SD. Key details to copy if missing: **L2-norm on q/k, β=σ(·), gate from x with A_log/dt_bias init, short conv on q/k/v, output RMSNorm and SiLU output gate.** | Small, if only details differ. |
| **DeltaProduct / RWKV-7** | Several Householder (delta) steps per token, or a generalized delta with vector-valued in-context LR. | Siems et al. 2025 (2502.10297). Peng et al. RWKV-7 2025 (2503.14456). | Better state tracking. Not targeted at mid-range LM loss. Low priority. | Medium. |
| **Gated Slot Attention** | A bounded-memory set of m slots with GLA-style gating, read via softmax over slots ("two-layer GLA linked by softmax"). | Zhang, Yang et al. NeurIPS'24, 2409.07146 (v). Better in-context recall than GLA at the same state. | **Closest published relative of your slot memory.** Worth reading for its slot-read softmax and forget parameterization (α as a sigmoid with a damping/temperature; **unsure of the exact constant**). | Read first. |
| **Output normalization of reads** | GroupNorm/RMSNorm per head on the read plus an output gate. TransNormer showed that denominator normalization is unstable and that norm-after works better. | Qin et al. 2022 (2210.10340). RetNet, GLA, GDN. | SG's running mean is already normalized. | **State expansion / multi-head** | Bigger state (HGRN2 outer-product expansion, Mamba2 heads). | HGRN2 2404.07904. Stuffed Mamba 2410.07145 (v): capacity is linear in state size. | Your m is already large (712/1280). More slots trade params and FLOPs. It doesn't fix horizon init. | Cheap knob (m), already explored. |

## Ranked top 5 to try first (15M, one seed, cheapest-first within impact)

1. **Re-parameterize and initialize the gate for long horizons.** Mamba2/GDN/Griffin/GLA style: g = softplus(lowrank(x) + b_slot), with b_slot = softplus⁻¹(r_slot) and r_slot log-uniform so that horizons 1/r span about 4–2000 tokens. Alternatively add the GLA temperature (÷16). Change only the gate. Run SG and SD once each, and compare the horizon histogram plus loss-by-distance at 64–1024. *Addresses (1) and (2) directly; tiny code; zero compute.*
2. **Document-boundary state reset**, and confirm how packing currently handles EOS. If memory spans documents, short horizons are the *correct* learned response. *Addresses (1); small; zero compute.*
3. **HGRN-style depth-increasing lower bound on λ** (a per-layer horizon floor). This formalizes what a few of your layers already found (150–350) and stops lower-layer-style collapse to 10–60 tokens in upper layers. It can be combined with #1 in one run if sweeps are limited, but then the effects are confounded. *Addresses (1) and (2); small.*
4. **TBTT / state passing across contiguous 1024 chunks, or train at 2–4K.** This gives gradient signal for horizons beyond the window, and more mid-range positions per token budget. *Addresses (1); small to medium code; no extra FLOPs.*
5. **GDN-standard I/O details: gate from x (not the address), short conv k=4 on the write/address path, and RMSNorm plus a SiLU output gate on reads** (Gated Attention, GLA, FoX-Pro). The short conv is the most likely to shift slot capacity from local to mid-range. *Addresses (2) and (1) indirectly; medium, because the conv state must be mirrored in kernel and run.c.*

Not in the top 5 but noted: a short-window (≤64–128) SWA hybrid (Based/SWAX) is the strongest known fix for the 64–1K gap. It changes the "one operator" design, so raise it with Gary before trying it.
